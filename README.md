Spanish healthcare system is universal. This is on paper great however this project sought to analyze the geographies of Spain along with the framework 5 pillars of geographic acccessibility specifically looking at Access.  The intial data pulled on average amount of people on waitlists for each community in Spain. I initially hypothesized that larger communities would inherently have longer wait times and we did see this trend in some cases however these numbers were varying after adjusting for population. Considering that each autonomous community sets its own policies in Spain, I assumed that data analysis would not really help understand access, but moreso the effectiveness of healthcare policy and it's affect on access. What initially started as a research assignment on in my geographies of healthcare class, turned into a much larger project, turned into a comparative study into a comparative case study of two communities and investigate why their healthcare access differs. I sought to try to answer 3 questions, to explain this, Does population size relate to healthcare accessibility?, How do healthcare access and waiting times differ between two Spanish autonomous communities?, though Spain offers universal healthcare system Are differences in healthcare outcomes associated with the political orientation of regional governments? Studying abroad in Madrid and having been across the countrly during independent travels this prject has strong personal connect, that I wanted to build on
